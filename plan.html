01 / One-page charter

Can adaptive imaging replace half the scan?

Build a stationary low-cost optical rig that estimates surface-defect density within 10% of an exhaustive scan while imaging at least 50% fewer fields of view.

Scientific targetSpatial defect density and texture variation on safe surface coupons
Primary instrumentFixed camera, controlled illumination, calibrated XY sample positioning
Ground truthFull raster scan plus scale/resolution target and repeated captures
Adaptive actionSelect next region, repeat, recalibrate, or stop
BaselineUniform raster with identical image count and fixed ordering
Core deliverableTraceable data, failure flags, uncertainty, and fixed-vs-adaptive comparison

Minimum success criteria

  • Pixel scale and field position remain within 2% across calibration checks.
  • Repeated defect-density estimates vary by no more than 5% on a fixed sample.
  • Bad focus, saturation, or illumination drift is detected in at least 90% of seeded failures.
  • Adaptive sampling reaches within 10% of exhaustive-scan defect density using at least 50% fewer images.
  • All raw images retain sample ID, position, settings, calibration version, timestamp, and quality flags.
02 / Modality selection

Three credible starts.

ModalityQuestion it can answerCalibrationSamples / ground truthDecision
Optical microscopyWhere are defects or texture changes, and how much of the surface must be scanned?Scale, resolution target, distortion, focus, flat-field, lighting drift, stage repeatabilityResolution target; patterned or intentionally marked safe coupons; full raster scanStart here
Visible/NIR reflectanceCan spectral response separate known finishes, coatings, or states?Dark signal, white reference, channel response, geometry, integration time, driftStable color/reflectance references plus labeled coupons; trusted comparison instrument if availableSecond modality
Electrical impedanceCan frequency response distinguish approved films or device states?Open, short, reference R/C loads, fixture parasitics, contact repeatability, temperatureKnown RC standards first; conductive films/devices only with permission and independent ground truthAccess-dependent

Why microscopy first: it provides an intuitive spatial decision problem, safe calibration artifacts, direct exhaustive ground truth, and a clear reason for later motion automation.

04 / Twelve weeks

Evidence gates before complexity.

WEEKS 1–2

Freeze the question

Confirm sample set, safety boundary, existing equipment, ground truth, and data permissions. Write the schema and acceptance tests before assembly.

Gate: one approved sample set + measurement protocol
WEEKS 3–4

Calibrate the stationary rig

Measure scale, resolution, focus behavior, illumination uniformity, repeatability, and drift. Seed bad-focus and saturation cases.

Gate: ≤2% scale drift; ≥90% seeded-failure detection
WEEKS 5–6

Build the exhaustive baseline

Capture full raster scans, version calibration, and create defect/texture labels with provenance. Repeat samples across days.

Gate: ≤5% repeated estimate variation
WEEKS 7–8

Add uncertainty

Implement a simple interpretable baseline and calibrated uncertainty. Evaluate held-out coupons and out-of-distribution flags.

Gate: confidence calibration reported, not assumed
WEEKS 9–10

Select the next view

Compare uniform, random, and uncertainty-guided spatial sampling on replayed data before automating motion.

Gate: within 10% of full scan using ≥50% fewer views
WEEKS 11–12

Integrate only what helps

Add stage or arm motion only if manual positioning limits coverage or repeatability. Publish failures and fixed-vs-adaptive results.

Gate: measurable benefit from motion, or defer it
